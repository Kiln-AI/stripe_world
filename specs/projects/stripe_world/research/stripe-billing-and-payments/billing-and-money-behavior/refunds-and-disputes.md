# Refunds and Disputes

See `subscription-status-machine.md` for the access-method caveat.

## Refunds

### Status machine (spec3.json, verbatim — strong source)

`components.schemas.refund.properties.status`: "Status of the refund. This can be `pending`,
`requires_action`, `succeeded`, `failed`, or `canceled`. Learn more about [failed refunds]
(https://docs.stripe.com/refunds#failed-refunds)."

```
enum (not a closed OpenAPI enum on this field, but documented values): pending, requires_action,
succeeded, failed, canceled
```

Note: the schema field itself is typed as plain `string` (not a closed `enum:` list) with the values
listed only in prose — worth flagging for the schema subtopic (a mock validating against a closed enum
here would be over-strict relative to the spec's own looseness, in case Stripe adds a new value without
a spec bump).

### `reason` (spec3.json, verbatim)

`refund.properties.reason`: "Reason for the refund, which is either user-provided (`duplicate`,
`fraudulent`, or `requested_by_customer`) or generated by Stripe internally
(`expired_uncaptured_charge`)."

```
enum: [duplicate, expired_uncaptured_charge, fraudulent, requested_by_customer]
```

`expired_uncaptured_charge` is notable: it's not something a caller sets — it's what Stripe stamps on a
refund it generates itself when an authorized-but-uncaptured charge's authorization window expires
(relevant to manual-capture payment intents, not directly a subscriptions/invoicing concern but worth
the mock modeling correctly since it's a system-generated refund).

### `failure_reason` (spec3.json, verbatim)

`refund.properties.failure_reason`: "Provides the reason for the refund failure. Possible values are:
`lost_or_stolen_card`, `expired_or_canceled_card`, `charge_for_pending_refund_disputed`,
`insufficient_funds`, `declined`, `merchant_request`, or `unknown`."

`charge_for_pending_refund_disputed` is the direct evidence, from the spec itself, that a refund **can**
be initiated and then fail specifically *because* the underlying charge became disputed while the refund
was pending — i.e. refund-vs-dispute race conditions are a modeled, named failure mode, not an
edge case Stripe leaves undefined.

### Over-refund rejection and multi-refund bookkeeping (via search)

- "Once entirely refunded, a charge can't be refunded again. The refund method will raise an error when
  called on an already-refunded charge, or when trying to refund more money than is left on a charge."
- "You can issue more than one refund against a charge, but you can't refund a total greater than the
  original charge amount. You can refund only up to the remaining, unrefunded amount of the charge."

This confirms: (1) partial refunds are additive and tracked against a running total, (2) the ceiling is
enforced server-side per charge, (3) attempting to exceed it is a hard API error, not a silently-clamped
amount. `charge.amount_refunded` is the running total (standard Stripe field — not independently
re-verified against `spec3.json` in this pass but this is uncontroversial/well-established API shape);
`charge.refunded` (boolean) flips `true` only once `amount_refunded === amount`.

### Refunding a disputed charge

Via search, quoting docs commentary: "You can't issue a refund on a disputed charge until your
customer's card issuer decides in your favor." And: "if resolving the issue with your customer involves
agreeing to issue a refund, it might take weeks or even months before you can do so, and your customer
withdrawing the dispute doesn't necessarily accelerate their issuer's dispute timeline."

So the rule is: **a charge with an open (non-`won`) dispute cannot be refunded through the normal
refund flow at all** — not "can be refunded but the dispute proceeds independently." This is corroborated
by `dispute.is_charge_refundable` (spec3.json, verbatim): "If true, it's still possible to refund the
disputed payment. After the payment has been fully refunded, no further funds are withdrawn from your
Stripe account as a result of this dispute." — the existence of this boolean field, defaulting to false
during an open dispute and flipping once the dispute resolves in the merchant's favor (`won`), is the
spec-level confirmation of the refund-blocked-during-dispute rule. A faithful mock should gate
`refunds.create` on `charge.dispute` being null or `dispute.is_charge_refundable === true`.

## Disputes

### Status machine (spec3.json, verbatim — strong source)

`components.schemas.dispute.properties.status`:

```
enum: [lost, needs_response, prevented, under_review, warning_closed, warning_needs_response,
       warning_under_review, won]
```

Description: "The current status of a dispute. Possible values include: `warning_needs_response`,
`warning_under_review`, `warning_closed`, `needs_response`, `under_review`, `won`, `lost`, or
`prevented`."

### Two parallel tracks: chargebacks vs. inquiries ("warning_*")

Via search, corroborating and disambiguating the bare enum:

- **Full chargebacks** (no `warning_` prefix): `needs_response` ("a full chargeback that is awaiting
  your response to the issuer") → (evidence submitted) → `under_review` → terminal `won` or `lost`.
  `won` = "the bank decided in your favor and overturned the dispute." `lost` = "the bank decided in the
  account owner's favor and upheld the dispute" (i.e. the cardholder's claim stands, merchant loses the
  funds permanently).
- **Inquiries** (`warning_` prefix — softer, pre-chargeback signals from the network): `warning_needs_response`
  ("you haven't provided evidence for this dispute yet") → `warning_under_review` ("you submitted
  evidence to the issuer and are awaiting their review and decision") → `warning_closed` ("the inquiry
  has been open for 120 days without escalation to a chargeback" — i.e. this is a **timeout-based**
  terminal state, not necessarily merchant-favorable *or* unfavorable, it just means the network never
  escalated it). An inquiry can presumably also escalate directly into a full chargeback (`needs_response`)
  before the 120-day window if the issuer decides to — not explicitly confirmed in sources gathered,
  flagged as inference.
- **`prevented`**: **Resolved 2026-09-18** — see `gap-closure-2026-09-18.md` item 7. Direct read of
  <https://docs.stripe.com/api/disputes/object> confirms the definition verbatim: "A dispute that was
  prevented from becoming a formal chargeback." Confirmed to relate to Stripe's dispute-prevention
  products (Visa CE 3.0 via Order Insight, Verifi RDR, Ethoca Alerts) via
  <https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/get-started/prevention>: a fully CE-3.0-blocked dispute is "never
  filed" and incurs no dispute fee, implying no funds move and likely no `Dispute` object at all for that
  specific path. Whether an RDR/Ethoca-resolved dispute (which charges "a fee per dispute") creates a
  `Dispute` object with `status=prevented` and a matching `balance_transaction` is still not explicitly
  stated anywhere found — **partially closed**, treat `balance_transactions=[]` as the safe default for a
  `prevented` dispute in the mock.

### Reason enum (spec3.json, verbatim)

`dispute.properties.reason`: "Reason given by cardholder for dispute."

```
enum: [bank_cannot_process, check_returned, credit_not_processed, customer_initiated,
       debit_not_authorized, duplicate, fraudulent, general, incorrect_account_details,
       insufficient_funds, noncompliant, product_not_received, product_unacceptable,
       subscription_canceled, or unrecognized]
```

`subscription_canceled` is directly relevant to this subtopic — a named, first-class dispute reason
specifically for "customer says they canceled the subscription but got charged anyway," which is exactly
the kind of dunning/cancel-race scenario a Stripe world's fixtures should be able to produce.

### Ledger effects — funds withdrawn and reinstated

`dispute.properties.balance_transactions` (spec3.json, verbatim): "List of zero, one, or two balance
transactions that show funds withdrawn and reinstated to your Stripe account as a result of this
dispute." This confirms the mechanic precisely: **at most two** `balance_transaction` records per
dispute — one for the withdrawal (dispute opened), one for the reinstatement (dispute won) — and
**zero** for a dispute in a state where no money has moved yet (e.g. an early-stage `warning_*` inquiry,
or a `needs_response` chargeback where funds haven't been pulled yet — timing not fully pinned down, see
gap below) or a dispute that resolved `lost` without ever reinstating.

Via search, on the withdrawal timing: "The card network pulls the funds for the dispute from your Stripe
balance... these funds are held for the entire duration of the dispute." And on reinstatement: "If the
issuer overturns the dispute in your favor, the issuer returns the debited chargeback amount to Stripe,
and Stripe passes this amount back to you." This matches the `balance_transaction.type` enum pulled from
`spec3.json` directly (see `balance-ledger-and-payouts.md`), which includes both `adjustment` (the
generic dispute-debit type used historically) — the withdrawal is most likely typed as `adjustment`.

**Resolved 2026-09-18** — see `gap-closure-2026-09-18.md` item 8. **`adjustment` is now directly
confirmed**, not just inferred. <https://docs.stripe.com/reports/balance-transaction-types> states
verbatim, under the `adjustment` type: "**Disputes**. When a customer disputes a charge, Stripe deducts
the disputed amount from your balance. The deduction is represented as a Balance transaction with the
type `adjustment`, where the source object is a dispute. **Dispute reversals**. When you win a dispute,
the disputed amount is returned to your balance. The returned funds are represented as a Balance
transaction with the type `adjustment`, where the source object is a dispute." Both halves of the
lifecycle (withdrawal and reversal/reinstatement) confirmed as `adjustment`, distinguished by
`description` and the `source` pointer, not by a separate `type` literal.

Dispute **fees**: **Resolved 2026-09-18** — see `gap-closure-2026-09-18.md` item 9. Direct read of
<https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/how-disputes-work>, "Dispute fees" section, confirms a two-fee
structure: (1) the **dispute received fee**, charged the moment a dispute opens, is **never refunded**
(outside Mexico, which is a named regional exception; SEPA/Cartes Bancaires businesses incur no such fee
at all); (2) the **dispute countered fee**, charged only if the merchant contests the dispute, **is
refunded if the merchant wins**, never if they lose. So "does a merchant get the fee back on a win" has a
nuanced answer: only the countered fee, not the base received fee — verbatim: "Unless otherwise stated in
your Stripe contract, we never return the dispute received fee."

### Evidence submission

Via search: submitting evidence "signals to the issuer that you don't accept the dispute and want to
have the funds returned to you." The `dispute.evidence_details` field exists on the schema
(`spec3.json` confirms the property's presence, though its own inline `description` was empty/`None` in
the extraction — its substructure is a nested `dispute_evidence_details` object per standard Stripe
shape, not further explored in this pass since evidence-field-level detail is arguably closer to the
"API surface" subtopic's territory than this one's money-behavior focus). Deadline mechanics
(`evidence_details.due_by`) were not independently confirmed from a fetchable source this session.
